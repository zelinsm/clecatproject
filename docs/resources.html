FAQs

What do I do if I see a cat outside?

In most cases, a cat simply being outside is not an emergency. However, all community cats should be sterilized through trap-neuter-return (TNR). Please see the resource directory below for low-cost spay/neuter resources and information about trapping. Friendly cats can be adopted into homes on your own or through local rescues.

What do I do if I see many cats outside?

Fill out our Request Form. We prioritize requests for overpopulated colonies, where caretakers are actively engaged, or where there are sick or injured cats. We manage over 80 colonies of nearly 1,000 community cats total around Cleveland. If you need assistance with trapping at your own residence or backyard, we can provide resources or advice, but we are rarely able to provide direct assistance. We encourage residents to rent a trap and trap on their own. Our efforts are best directed toward trapping large, unmanaged colonies where community cat welfare is at its lowest.

What if the cats are sick or injured?

If you haven’t already, fill out our Request Form to seek help from our organization. For urgent cases (such as if a cat was hit by a car), we recommend taking the cat to the closest emergency veterinary hospital:

  • MedVet Cleveland West – 24 hours • 216-362-6000 • 20400 Emerald Pkwy, Cleveland, OH 44135
  • Metropolitan Veterinary Hospital – 24 hours • 440-673-3483 • 734 Alpha Dr, Highland Heights, OH 44143
  • West Park Animal Hospital – open until 1 am • 216-252-4500 • 4117 Rocky River Dr, Cleveland, OH 44135
  • VCA Great Lakes – 24 hours • 216-831-6789 • 4760 Richmond Rd, Warrensville Heights, OH 44128

What if the cats appear pregnant?

Cats who appear pregnant are currently accepted for spays at the Cleveland APL without an appointment, Monday-Friday 7:45-8:45 AM.

I found kittens outside. What do I do?

Don’t remove them right away. Fill out our Found Kittens form if you are looking for guidance about kittens, or our Request Form if you are looking for direct assistance.

How do I get feral cats removed from my property?

You don’t. Healthy feral cats should not be removed from their territory. The most effective and humane approach is Trap-Neuter-Return (TNR). When cats are removed from an area, new unsterilized cats move in and begin breeding. This is known as the vacuum effect. Cleveland Community Cat Project does not assist with relocating or euthanizing healthy feral cats.

I took in a community cat. Now what?

We encourage you to review our Guidance for Requesting Help from Rescues prior to reaching out to nonprofits. The guide explains how to work with rescues to give your cat the best possible chance of receiving help.

What if I need to surrender my personal cat due to financial difficulties?

We cannot intake owned cats, but we are happy to help connect you with resources so you and your cat can remain together. We encourage owners to exhaust every reasonable option before surrendering an animal. If you must temporarily or permanently surrender your cat, the resource directory below contains a list of other local rescues and shelters.

What if I need to surrender my cat who is urinating inappropriately?

We understand how difficult and overwhelming these situations can be. It is a leading cause of surrender and euthanasia. In most cases, it can be resolved with a structured, persistent approach. Even if you think you’ve tried everything, please read our Guidance for Feline House-Soiling before surrendering or euthanizing your cat.

Trap-Neuter-Return (TNR)

Trap-Neuter-Return (TNR) is the most effective and humane way to manage community cat populations. Spaying and neutering helps prevent fighting, disease, and suffering among outdoor cats.

Surrendering a Cat: Foster-Based Rescues and Shelters

We have no formal partnership or agreements with the following organizations. Inclusion in this list does not constitute endorsement.

Rescues and shelters across Northeast Ohio are at or near capacity year-round. Please consider every possible alternative before surrendering a cat. If you are able, foster the cat through a rescue or rehome the cat privately using adoption platforms or social media. Our organization strongly discourages surrendering cats unless absolutely necessary. Rescues and shelters are overwhelmed, and surrender should always be a last resort. If possible, foster or rehome directly through a reputable rescue. Please review these additional tips provided by Humane World for Animals. 

Never surrender feral cats to shelters or barn home programs. Feral cats should always be trapped, fixed, and returned to their original territory. We do not support barn home programs under any circumstances.

Caring for Community Cats Outdoors

The best way to care for community cats is to ensure all cats are fixed and vaccinated (FVRCP and Rabies). In addition to sterilization and vaccination, providing food, water, and shelter helps community cats thrive. Our organization recycles Styrofoam coolers to make insulated cat shelters. You can request one using this link for a small donation: Insulated Cat House Request Form. You can also make your own using a plastic tote and straw (not hay or blankets!): Alley Cat Allies - DIY Cat Shelter (video).

Lost or Missing Cats in Cleveland

If your cat is lost, act quickly. Most cats remain close to home and can be found with prompt, consistent effort. Follow these steps to improve your chances of a safe reunion.

1. Search Your Immediate Area. Begin looking as soon as you notice your cat is missing. Most lost cats are hiding within a few houses’ distance. Check under porches, in garages, sheds, crawl spaces, and dense shrubs. Go out at dusk or dawn when it’s quiet, and softly call your cat’s name while listening for meows.

2. Use Familiar Scents to Help Guide Them Home. Place your cat’s litter box, bedding, or favorite blanket outside to help them recognize home by smell. Leave out food only if you are monitoring it, as it can attract wildlife.

3. Notify Local Animal Agencies. Contact or visit nearby shelters and animal control facilities to report your cat missing.

  • Cleveland Division of Animal Care & Control – 216-664-3069

  • Cleveland Animal Protective League (APL) –clevelandapl.org

Bring a clear photo and your contact information. Continue checking back frequently. Many shelters receive new intakes daily.

4. Post on Social Media and Local Networks. Share your cat’s photo, name, sex, spay/neuter status, microchip status, identifying features, date they were lost, and last known location on these local Facebook groups and community platforms:

  • PawBoost

  • Petco Love Lost

  • Lost Pets Cleveland, OH

  • Sam the Parrot – Lost & Found Pets NE Ohio

  • Nextdoor

  • Post on neighborhood Facebook groups and Ring

5. Create Flyers and Talk to Neighbors. Distribute flyers within several blocks of your home. Include your cat’s photo, description, and your contact number. Ask neighbors to check garages and sheds—many cats get accidentally trapped inside. Ask neighbors to check cameras.

6. Continue Checking and Expanding the Search. Cats may hide for several days before emerging, so keep searching even if time passes. Revisit areas at night and refresh online posts every few days to stay visible.
